## 2. Critical research insights

- **Adaptive speculation is a Serving control problem**: TableTune is especially concrete because it measures the actual vLLM step on a specific GPU and uses accept-count feedback. AgentSpec adds workflow-structure-aware token budgets, while Domino requires a trained parallel drafter; conflating all three as the same control mechanism would be wrong.
- **Cache quality vs execution are coupled**: Raincheck progressively restores context after returning the first answer; KV Admission decides what to write; A3 selectively repairs/fuses reused context; Seer learns retrieval indexes; Beyond and SampKV alter KV representations. All six change state access or precision. Their accuracy/exactness and temporal behavior are different, and **none is established as a lossless drop-in on Ascend**.
- **Batch state can be request-owned**: Orthrus treats embedding and generation as heterogeneous compute classes. RW-TTT adds owner/version/read-write state transition constraints for per-request mutable weights. These are separate reasons classical continuous batching can fail.
- **Hardware portability is a research question, not a free conclusion**: GPU-resident WebGPU scheduling yields a distinct browser driver/CPU-readback contract, not a shortcut to CUDA, Ascend or online PD-serving optimizations.
- **Prediction benchmarking needs a physical lower bound**: output-length MAE conflates predictor error and autoregressive sampling variance, so source comparisons must hold sampler, target model and dataset fixed.
